How the spike decodes (a prototype for `Tsuki.Media`):

- **Input.** bgm.hed record 33 is read with a small HED8 reader (`GameFiles.cs`) (containers.md 2.1-2.3), and
  the entry with one positional read of bgm.mrg (read-only).
- **Header.** The RIFF header is parsed in C# with the strict checks of `parse_wave`.
- **No demuxer, no AVIO.** The `atrac9` decoder is opened with `block_align` = 512 and with
  12 bytes of extradata = fmt + 0x28 (dwVersionInfo, config word, reserved). These are the
  bytes FFmpeg's wav demuxer passes; `atrac9dec` requires exactly 12.
- **Packets.** Each superframe of the data chunk is sent as one non-refcounted packet of
  512 B. The FLTP output planes are interleaved by plain copies.
- **Contract.** The result is an `AudioClip` (Tsuki.Engine Contracts) through an
  `IAudioDecoder` implementation (`FfmpegAudioDecoder`). Samples = `decoded[256 : 256 + n]`;
  loop = `[smpl.start - 256, smpl.end - 256]`.
- **Mixer.** One voice reads the clip and jumps from `loop.End` (inclusive) to `loop.Start`
  (ARCHITECTURE 7.2), with gain 1 and the engine pan law at pan 0.5 (gL = gR = 1). The mix is
  float32 stereo, rendered in periods of <= 1024 frames, never clipped.
- **SDL.** `SDL_OpenAudioDeviceStream(default playback, F32 2 ch 48 kHz, get-callback)`. The
  callback renders the mixer on SDL's audio thread; a postmix callback captures what SDL hands
  to the driver.

The postmix capture is the buffer SDL hands to WASAPI. Its bits equal the oracle's render
across every seam, so nothing between our mixer and the driver altered a sample. The +50 ppm
effective rate is the device clock measured against QPC.

**Latency.**

- **Measured.** SDL's buffer is 480 frames (10 ms), with a steady 10 ms callback cadence. The
  mixer fills a period in microseconds (worst 1.7 ms, once in 32,739 calls). The engine's grain
  is 1,024 frames (21.3 ms, audio.md 7), so this path is already finer than the PS4's.
- **Not measured.** The WASAPI engine and the device add their own buffering after the postmix
  point. End-to-end output latency needs a loopback capture, which SDL does not expose on
  Windows (open issue 3).
- **No smaller buffer.** `SDL_HINT_AUDIO_DEVICE_SAMPLE_FRAMES` does not shrink the WASAPI
  period in SDL 3.5.0.

## 5. Click analysis of the seam (data property, not a port defect)

The metrics are those of audio.md 2.4 (`scratch/audio/seam_jump.py`, `seam_test.py`), in int16
units, computed on the rendered output at the seam. The values are identical at both seams.

| measure | at the seam (end -> start) | natural continuation after the loop end | natural continuation into the loop start | with a 128-frame (2.67 ms) linear crossfade (not engine behaviour) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J = linear-extrapolation error / local median step | **15.6** | 0.81 | 0.48 | 0.45 |
| sample step L / R (dBFS) | -20.5 / -28.1 | -35.0 / -34.6 | -35.6 / -36.9 | - |
| HF energy > 19.5 kHz of a 512-frame window, share of the window energy | **-39.7 dB** | -136.4 dB | -128.4 dB | -102.2 dB |
| HF ratio vs unspliced windows at end-4096/-8192/-12288 (share -133.9 / -127.6 / -135.7 dB) | 4.2e9 | 0.83 | 3.4 | 1.7e3 |

Reading:

- **There is a step.** bgm #33 has a waveform step at its loop seam: J 15.6 is in audio.md's
  "> 10 = click" class, and the jump on the left channel is 0.094 (-20.5 dBFS).
- **It comes from the splice.** Both natural continuations are smooth (J < 1), and the codec
  leaves the band above 19.5 kHz empty (about -130 dB). So the -39.7 dB of HF energy at the
  seam is a broadband tick created by the splice itself.
- **It is in the data.** The loop points are the file's own, and the engine applies them
  without a crossfade (audio.md 2.3-2.4). The PS4 therefore plays the same discontinuity, and
  a faithful port reproduces it. The spike does.
- **A crossfade would hide it.** A 2.67 ms crossfade would bring J to 0.45, but the output would
  no longer equal the engine's. That is a product decision (open issue 1).
- **No listening test was done.** These are signal measurements; nobody has listened to the
  render. To listen, the seams are at 172.042 s and 324.042 s of `samples/spikes/s1/cs_loop3.wav`.

## 6. Engine-style loop (seek + decoder reset) vs the decoded-copy jump

The PS4 streams the BGM. On a wrap it re-seeks to superframe `loop_start / 1024`, clears the
decoder and drops 256 + `loop_start % 1024` samples (audio.md 2.3). The spike did the same
with FFmpeg: superframe 939, `avcodec_flush_buffers`, 720 samples dropped. It then compared
163,120 frames (3.4 s) with the decoded copy:

| variant | samples not bit-identical | max abs diff |
|---|---|---|
| warm decoder (decoded to the end), then reset and seek | 0 | 0 |
| fresh decoder started at superframe 939 | 0 | 0 |

So with libavcodec's atrac9, streaming plus seeking gives exactly the decoded-copy result at
this loop start. The 256-sample warm-up lies entirely inside the dropped 720 samples. A
streaming BGM player (63 MB less memory per track) would also be sample-exact here. This is
measured on this one seek point only.

## 8. Consequences for the port (proposals; stream E / lead)

1. **Decoder.** `Tsuki.Media`'s ATRAC9 decoder can be this one: no demuxer, 12-byte extradata,
   one packet per superframe. It is bit-identical to the oracle and decodes about 1,500x real
   time on one core. A 172 s track takes 0.11 s, so decoding a whole BGM before playback costs
   less than one 60 Hz frame budget of wall time on a worker.
2. **Test boundary.** Sample-exact tests belong at the mixer output or the SDL postmix point.
   On a device whose mix format is not F32 48 kHz (a 44.1 kHz device, or SDL's disk driver),
   SDL converts, and only the content up to SDL can be compared. T-tests that need SDL can use
   `SDL_CreateAudioStream` in pull mode, as in section 7.
3. **Underruns.** At the default 10 ms WASAPI period the callback never ran short over
   801 s of playback (4 runs), and its worst cost was 1.7 ms. Rendering the mix directly in
   the SDL callback from a decoded buffer is enough for one BGM voice. The full mixer (2 BGM +
   20 SE + voice) still has to be measured in T7.

## 10. Open issues

1. **Seam crossfade (product decision).** bgm #33's seam has a measured step (J 15.6, HF share
   -39.7 dB) that the PS4 plays too. An optional 2-5 ms crossfade would remove it
   (J 0.45), but output would then differ from the engine and from the golden render.
   Default: engine-exact. Decide whether an option exists (audio.md 2.4 already suggests one).
2. **FFmpeg licence wording.** The BtbN DLLs report `avcodec_license()` = "LGPL version 3 or
   later" (a `--enable-version3` build). ARCHITECTURE / release notes that say LGPL 2.1
   should be checked by the lead.
3. **End-to-end latency is not measured.** SDL shows a 10 ms buffer and 10 ms cadence, but
   the WASAPI engine and device latency after the postmix point are unknown. Measuring them
   needs a WASAPI loopback capture outside SDL, or a hardware loopback.
4. **SDL 3.5.0 quirks for Tsuki.Platform.**
   - The disk driver ignores the requested spec and the format hints, so it cannot be used
     for headless sample-exact tests. Use stream pull mode or the postmix capture instead.
   - `SDL_HINT_AUDIO_DEVICE_SAMPLE_FRAMES` does not change the WASAPI period (480 frames).
5. **Where the results live.** The ROADMAP and `docs/status/spikes.md` expect the S1 numbers
   in the `spikes.md` S1 table, and WAV dumps in `artifacts/M0/spikes/`. This task put them
   in this file and in `samples/spikes/s1/`. The owner of `spikes.md` should copy the table
   or link here.
6. **The spike references `Tsuki.Engine`.** It uses the `AudioClip` / `AudioLoop` /
   `IAudioDecoder` contracts, which ProjectRulesTests allows for spikes. A contract change
   breaks the spike build, so it should be adapted or deleted with it.
