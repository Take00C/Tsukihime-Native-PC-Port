# K33: EXIT on the title cut from the game's own menu art

Package K33 (label `k33`), run on 2026-10-30 on top of commit 9a9a227 plus the uncommitted K30 scrub (untouched). Nothing is committed; the new files are staged
with `git add` so the public copy picks them up. Builds in `artifacts/k33` (Release, `-warnaserror`, 0 warnings). Every game run hidden and muted (`--hidden --mute
--fast`, `SDL_AUDIO_DRIVER=dummy`) on its own `--save-dir` under `scratch/k33/app/`, from a copy of the built game folder (`scratch/k33/exe`). No window was shown.
`artifacts/play*`, `artifacts/dist`, the user's `%APPDATA%\Tsuki` (its `save.dat` is dated 17:21, before this package), `%APPDATA%\shadPS4` and the game folder
(read-only) were not touched. No knife code was translated (`TitleExitArt.cs` is the port's own; `docs/THIRD_PARTY.md` is unchanged and its test is green). No
PowerShell with Add-Type, DllImport, P/Invoke, LoadLibrary, base64 or Invoke-Expression; the pictures were measured with Pillow and numpy from `.venv`.

## 1. What the user asked and what was found

The title's sixth item EXIT (package K18) was drawn with the game's glyph font: the right size and colours, but a different face (the game's sans or serif-like
text face, heavier) than the five items CONTINUE / NEW GAME / LOAD GAME / CONFIG / GALLERY, which are pictures in TITLE_PARTS child 0 (968 x 472; 296 x 72 cells
in three columns: normal, cursor, pressed; docs/formats/system_flow.md 4.4 and 4.5, archives_ui_movies.md 5.2). No cell spells EXIT and no word has an X.

What the art is (measured on the sheet, both columns, `scratch/k33/art/cells_*.png`): capitals on rows 28..46 of the cell (19 px; the serifs' anti-aliasing
touches rows 27 and 47), a thick-thin serif face (stems 2 px at full alpha, hairlines 1 px), letters 9..12 px apart (median 11), each word centred on
column 147..148 of its cell. Normal column: white core (255, 255, 255) at alpha 218, thin strokes at 168 / 132, a one-pixel dark edge (alpha 54..118, RGB 2..150,
stronger above and below the letters than beside them), then a glow whose colour runs from dark blue (74, 99, 144) at 2 px to light blue (194, 240, 255) from
10 px on and whose alpha falls 50 -> 2 over 27 px. Cursor column: cyan core (15..22, 214..228, 246..250) at alpha 254, a cyan glow (2, 221, 255) at alpha 22 -> 2
over 20 px, no dark edge. The glow is not simply additive: in an 11 px gap the midpoint has alpha 16..18 in the cursor column (about twice the single-letter
value at 5.5 px) but 28..34 in the normal column (about 1.3 times). CONFIG sits one row higher in the sheet than the other four words (ink 27..45).

## 2. The change: `TitleExitArt` composes the cell at run time (`src/Tsuki.Ui/Screens/TitleExitArt.cs`)

**Letters.** Each cell is split into letters by its alpha columns: a column with any pixel at alpha >= 128 (`Ink`: the cores are 218 / 254, the dark edge and the
glow stay under 120) is letter, a run of such columns is a letter, dips of up to 5 columns (the counters of C, O, G) are joined; the five words must give 7, 8, 6,
7 and 8 letters or the composer gives up. The letters keep their rows, so the baseline is the other words' baseline.

- **E**: the crispest of NEW GAME[1], LOAD GAME[7], CONTINUE[7] among the words on CONTINUE's rows (crispness = the heaviest column's alpha sum). The E of
  NEW GAME was rasterised at another sub-pixel phase (its stem is 168 / 132 over two columns instead of 218 / 218), so the rule picks LOAD GAME's (4238 vs
  CONTINUE's 4210 in the normal column; 4786 vs 4742 and NEW GAME's 4756 in the cursor column).
- **I**: CONTINUE[4] (CONFIG's I is excluded by the row rule: it would float one pixel high). **T**: CONTINUE[3] (the only T).
- **X**: from the Y of GALLERY (GALLERY[6]). Its upper half is a V whose left arm is thick and right arm thin, as the face's diagonals are. Rows above the letter's
  centre row (37) are the Y's; every row below is the **point mirror** (rows and columns reversed about the centre) of the row above, and the centre row takes the
  brighter of its own pixels and their mirror, so the two diagonals cross without a seam and the thick stroke runs top-left to bottom-right, the thin one top-right
  to bottom-left, with the serifs of the Y's arms on all four ends. A plain vertical flip (the literal wording of the request) was built too and read at 3x
  (`scratch/k33/proto/strip_c1_flip_3x.png`): it puts both thick strokes on the left ("<" thick, ">" thin), which no X of this face has; for a symmetric V the
  two constructions are the same picture. The point mirror is what ships.

**Layout.** The ink widths E 14, X 18, I 7, T 17 with the median gap 11 between them (89 px), centred where the five words are centred (mean 147.5): the letters
land on columns 103..116, 128..145, 157..163, 175..191 of the cell (centre 147.0; the five words sit at 147.0..148.0). Rows 27..47 as CONTINUE's.

**Glow.** A letter's glow in its own word mixes with its neighbours' and depends on the letter beside it, so the glow is not cut with the letter: each letter is
taken as its box (ink columns and rows plus one pixel of margin: its anti-aliasing and the dark edge) and the glow around the four boxes is **synthesized from the
same sheet**. `GlowModel`, measured on the five words of the column: for every offset (dx, dy) from a pixel to the nearest ink pixel of the nearest letter, the
mean alpha and alpha-weighted colour of the pixels that have no second letter within 20 px (the single-letter kernel, exact on the pixel grid, so the dark edge
above and below, the lighter halo beside and the colour by distance come out as the art has them; an offset nobody sampled takes the mean of its distance ring),
and one number `Sum` fitted by least squares on every pixel with two letters near: the weaker of the two letters' glows is added to the stronger with that weight
(0.51 in the normal column, 1.16 in the cursor column). Painting a pixel = the kernel at its offset to the nearest letter, plus `Sum` times the kernel at the
offset to the second nearest, with the colour of the stronger. Nothing is hard-coded but the sheet's geometry: another player's sheet gives its own letters and
its own glow. The check of the model (`TitleExitArt.RepaintGlow`, printed by `Tsuki.Cli exit-art`): each word's own glow repainted from its letter boxes against
the sheet's cell, outside the boxes: normal column mean alpha error 1.02..1.61 per pixel (NEW GAME 1.49, LOAD GAME 1.61, CONFIG 1.02, GALLERY 1.26, CONTINUE 1.55),
1.0..2.1 % of the pixels off by more than 8, max 18..21 (one pixel of LOAD GAME 47); cursor column mean 0.68..1.06, at most 0.5 % off by more than 8, max 8..11.
The real glow varies with the letter shapes by about that much, so the composed glow is the art's mean glow, not one letter's.

**Fallback.** Another sheet size, a word that does not split into its letters, GALLERY on other rows than CONTINUE, a Y not symmetric about the middle row, or a
word that would not fit: `Compose` returns null and the title keeps K18's glyph-font EXIT (unchanged, tested).

**Plumbing.** The composed cells are run-time textures, never written anywhere (they are the player's art): `TextureKind.UiComposed` (7; A = `UiComposedId`,
B = language, C = version) and `TextureRef.ForUiComposed` in the engine contract; `UiComposedPicture` and `UiComposedId` (`TitleExit` 0, `TitleExitCursor` 1) in
`Tsuki.Ui/Framework/UiComposed.cs`; `UiAssets.Composed(id[, language])` builds both looks once per language from `IGameData.UiSheet(TitleParts, lang, 0)` and
drops them with the language; `UiPainter.Composed(id, src, dst, alpha, blend)` emits one quad through the same `Add` path as `Sprite` (the painter's alpha and
clip apply, so the staggered fade-in, the idle fade and the cursor swap treat it like the other cells); `TitleScreen.DrawMenu` draws EXIT with it and falls back
to `DrawExitLabel`. The three texture resolvers: `TextureFeeder.Composed` (the game; `GameApp` wires it to the UI's assets, uploaded at once like a thumbnail),
`UiTextureUploader` in `ui-shot`, and `GameTextureProvider` (the story-effect path, which does not draw menus) lists the kind among those it does not serve.
`UiPreview` has the scene **`title-exit`** (the cursor on EXIT), so the cursor look is in `ui-shot` and in the real-data theories. New command **`Tsuki.Cli
exit-art [--lang xx] [--out DIR]`** writes the two composed cells, the five source cells and a strip of the six items at 1:1 and 3x into `scratch/exit-art`
(git-ignored) and prints the layout and the model check.

Measured where it matters: with the six-row layout the cell is drawn 1:1 at (816, 952.25) for EXIT (fractional rows for items 1, 2, 3 and 5, as K28 left them:
the GPU filters EXIT and its neighbours alike).

## 5. PS4 versus Switch

The PS4 has five items and no EXIT; the six-row menu and this cell are the port's own. The composed cell is made of the PS4's own letters, so it is the same art
the PS4 shows for the other items.

## 6. Open issues

1. The glow around EXIT is the sheet's mean glow per offset, not a copy of one letter's glow: the real art's glow varies with the letter shapes by up to about
   10 alpha near the letters (section 2, the model check). A pixel-for-pixel glow is not possible for letters that never stood next to each other.
2. The composed cell was looked at in crops of hidden renders and runs, not on a monitor (rule). The GPU draws it 1:1 at the item's slot, which has fractional
   rows for four of the six items (K28); EXIT and its neighbours are filtered alike.
3. The composition runs on the first title draw of a run (or after a language switch) on the UI thread: five letter fields per word, the kernel and the fit.
   Its time was not measured separately; the hidden runs show no extra waiting (6..7 frames, 25..32 ms in all, as K28's runs).
4. TITLE_PARTS child 0 is the same picture in all four languages, so the composition is too; the texture key still carries the language, so a sheet that differed
   would get its own cell.
5. `Tsuki.Cli exit-art` writes the player's art (the cells) into `scratch/`, which is git-ignored and refused by the pre-commit guard; the pictures of this package
   stay there and are referred to by path only.
